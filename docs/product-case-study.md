# Product case study

## Context

Many beginner language products separate vocabulary, grammar and reading into independent exercises. That makes progress easy to count, but it often postpones contact with real writing. FrenchBridge explores a different question: can a beginner approach original French early if the support is sequenced carefully?

## Product goal

Help English-speaking learners build close-reading confidence through short attributed passages, official French media and increasingly independent responses over 24 weeks.

This is deliberately presented as an A1–B1 reading path rather than a complete general-French course. Speaking, live correction and broad conversational coverage remain outside its strongest scope.

## Learning loop

1. **Try** — Read the French source and answer a small evidence-based question.
2. **Reveal** — Use a project-written English aid to confirm meaning.
3. **Return** — Revisit the French before marking the unit mastered.
4. **Recall** — Review source vocabulary later with explicit scheduling choices.
5. **Respond** — Save a short written interpretation or summary.

The central product decision is that completion must represent evidence of learning. A reading unit cannot be mastered before its check is answered correctly; media and writing units also require their own meaningful evidence.

## First-time and returning users

The first visit explains the method through a short, optional Try → Reveal → Recall sequence. After a learner begins, the home screen changes into a Today dashboard showing:

- the next unit;
- source terms due for review;
- daily reading, recall and response progress;
- overall path mastery;
- any saved draft ready to resume.

This avoids making returning learners repeatedly pass through a marketing page.

## Content authority

Every unit identifies its authority:

- public-domain French literature for reproduced primary excerpts;
- RFI and TV5MONDE links for contemporary public-service media;
- the Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume for capability framing;
- visibly labelled project-written English translations and prompts.

External media is linked rather than copied. This keeps source ownership clear and prevents official material from being presented as project-created content.

## Key constraints

- The product needed to run as a static website without a paid application server.
- Guest learners needed to retain progress without creating an account.
- Existing saved progress could not be lost during the learning-state redesign.
- The same learner could edit progress or drafts from multiple devices.
- The mobile experience needed to work at 390px without becoming a compressed desktop layout.

## Outcome

The resulting product combines a complete navigable reading path with a meaningful daily return loop, scheduled recall, draft persistence and optional cross-device synchronization. The strongest learning and engineering decisions are documented separately in the public architecture and reduced sample files.
